§ 01 / Abstract — 001

The protocol of a research laboratory, applied to software engagements that cannot afford to fail.

n.b. see fig. 01 for operator topology.

We treat software as a discipline, not a service.

Most outsourced software fails because the team was assembled before the problem was understood. We invert that. Every engagement begins with observation, proceeds through deliberate assembly, and concludes with measurable outcomes — the structure of a laboratory experiment, applied to production systems.

The right team, matched to the right shape of problem, will outperform a larger team assembled on headcount alone.

Our work is documented, peer-reviewed, and held to standards more familiar to research than to consulting. We publish our methods, defend our findings, and archive each engagement as a reproducible case file.

§ 02 / Methods — 002

Four phases, executed in strict sequence. Deviation triggers review.

The laboratory protocol.

No templates — each project is scoped to its own constraints before assembly begins. The sequence is consistent; the composition is not.

01
Observation
Scope the system.
Constraints, architecture, and failure modes are documented before a line of code is written. Stakeholder interviews produce a project signature.
02
Assembly
Match the team.
Our network of 3,400+ operators is filtered by project signature. Composition is optimised against prior outcomes — not titles, not résumés.
03
Iteration
Run the experiment.
Tight cycles, daily visibility, and engineering leadership that holds the line on architectural decisions. Drift is flagged within 24 hours.
04
Delivery
Report the result.
Rigorous acceptance testing, measurable outcomes, documented handoff — or continued operation under one of our standing protocols.
